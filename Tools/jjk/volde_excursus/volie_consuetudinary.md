# JJK Consuetudinary — Heat Birth and Death

Read this before you call `jjx_create` or `jjx_archive` — every time, and not
only when the act feels doubtful. It is an **excursus** (`jjsuc_excursus`):
held out of the standing session load and read on demand, reached from the
"Heat Birth and Death Are the Operator's" stub in `Tools/jjk/volis_jjk.md`.

A consuetudinary is a religious house's book of customs — the ceremonies the
house performs, written down because they come round rarely enough that nobody
carries them by heart. Heat birth and heat death are acts of that kind: rare,
ceremonial, and reserved to one authority. The fence itself stays resident in
the conduct home where every session reads it whether or not this door is
opened; what the ceremony consists of, and what has talked sessions over the
fence before, is here.

Two pointers below resolve in that conduct home rather than in this file. "The
gazette table above" is its gazette wire-format table, which carries the
`jjezs_quittance` wire form. "The fence above" is the never-on-your-own-
initiative sentence that stayed there.

**The gate is on initiative, not on the verb.** You run either command the
moment you are directed to; what is barred is deciding *for yourself* that a
heat should exist or should end. Two things are direction: the operator says
so, naming the heat — sufficient in full, in chat, at the moment they say it —
or your own docket **names the act**, as the phase-ladder boundary pace does
when its slated work is cutting the successor heat (JJS0 "The Phase Ladder").
A docket that merely implies a heat is wanted is not direction, and a plan you
proposed in chat is not a docket however warmly it was received.

**This is not the blotter lock.** There the ceremony is a typed gate in the
operator's *terminal*, which an agent does not have, so "the operator authorized
it in chat" is barred there. Here the operator's word **is** the authority and
only acting without it is gated — discuss, propose, and draft heats freely. Do
not over-tighten by analogy.

**Recommending, so the stop has a path.** Enough for the operator to rule on in
one reply:

- the silks you would give it;
- the shape its paddock would carry — its sections (JJS0 `jjsp_paddock`), what it
  is for, and its done-line written in *checkable* clauses: each one names something
  a later session can sight in the store or in the trees and rule met or unmet from
  what it finds there (JJS0 "The Done-When");
- which paces would populate it, at least in outline;
- why no existing heat fits, naming the ones you weighed;
- offered *before* the call, never after — the heat exists the moment
  `jjx_create` returns, so creating-then-offering-to-undo asks the operator to
  ratify a fact rather than make a decision.

**Retirement is preceded by the audit, and the audit is a document you stage.**
A heat with every pace resolved reads done while its paddock's done-when can
still call for work nobody cut — not hypothetical and not rare: two heats stood
in that state on the day this gate was cut, both with every pace resolved.
A resolved board is not the question. Before `jjx_archive`, read the paddock's
done-when and rule on it **clause by clause** — each clause met with the
evidence that meets it, or named unmet with the work that was never done — and
stage that verdict as a lede-less `jjezs_quittance` notice (wire form in the
gazette table above). The door refuses without one, and the body lands in the
archive commit — the heat leaving the live store in the same act, so that commit
is the audit's only permanent home. The equerry enforces the artifact's existence
and reads the verdict never: whether a clause is genuinely met is your
determination, and no door will second-guess it or catch you skimping it.

**The auditor identifies; the operator disposes.** Report what you
found — the clause, and the work it calls for — and stop. The operator strips
the clause from the paddock through `jjx_curry`, or directs the work to be
slated, and either way the retirement waits on their word. An audit that
resolved its own finding would be a heat's shape decided by the session that
wanted it retired.

**A heat waiting on a ruling is stabled, not racing.** Where what remains of a heat
is a question for the operator rather than work anyone can cut, the honest state is
`stabled`, and the recommendation to make is the furlough. A racing heat with an empty
roster reads as done to every reader of the board, and any draw over done-seeming heats
reads racing ones by design — so a heat parked on a question presents itself for
retirement every time the board is looked at.

**Barred rationalizations**, each from the live incident this section repairs:
"this deferred question needs somewhere to live" (bank it in a paddock, an itch,
or a pace); "the next slot in the heat-name sequence was free" (a free slot is
not an invitation); "the operator approved the plan and the heat was in it"
(structural acts are approved by name, never by inclusion); "it is under the
size floor but it will grow" (a placeholder heat bets the operator's planning
surface on work that may never arrive); "I overreached, so I will archive my own
creation to tidy up" (retirement is the same fence — report it and let the
operator direct the remedy); "the done-when clause was stale anyway, so I
curried it out and retired" (stripping a clause is disposing of the finding,
which is the operator's act however obviously right the strip looks); "every
pace is resolved, so the audit is a formality" (that is the exact state both
field specimens were in).
