## Job Jockey Configuration

Job Jockey (JJ) is installed for managing project initiatives.

**A fact stated twice here is stated twice on purpose.** Homes are placed by *moment* — what the reader is doing when they reach one — so the vocabulary list, the slating guidance, the protocol verdict and the bridle dispositions each carry the part of a doctrine their own reader acts on, and each names the moment it serves. Before collapsing an apparent duplicate, name the moment each home fires: only where two fire at the same moment for the same reader is one of them spare. Parameter facts are the exception with a single home — the command reference — and prose that restates a row rather than pointing at it is spare by that test.

**Concepts** — what the words mean; what to *do* about them is in the protocol sections below:
- **Heat**: Bounded initiative with coherent goals that are clear and present (3-50 paces). Status: `racing` (active execution) or `stabled` (paused for planning). Location: `current/` or `retired/` (done).
- **Pace**: Discrete action within a heat.
- **Kind**: The durable axis beside the voidable bridle designation — what the work *is*, as the slater stated it. Three values — `hackamore`, `poort` and `entrepas` — in **one field**, so they are mutually exclusive by construction and a pace carries one or none. Two doors write it: `jjx_enroll` at birth, `jjx_whorl` thereafter. A reslate cannot void it, the plan write a redocket funnels through taking no kind. `entrepas` is the one exception to that two-doors law: it is declared at birth or never, the flip refusing it in both directions.
- **Lope**: A pace carrying the *hackamore* kind — work that completes in conversation with the operator rather than by execution. A lope stands *rough* and carries the kind: it holds no tier, effort, or warrant, and `jjx_apostille` refuses one. *Lope* is the only word for it in prose; `hackamore` is the token, and every param, field and serialized value keeps that spelling. Graduation is transmute-in-place: settle the thinking, redocket into executable form, flip the kind away, and the pace stands awaiting designation. Full doctrine: JJS0 "The Lope" and `jjdpk_hackamore`.
- **Poort**: The other kind — work that runs solo against shared durable state: the pace holds the pass, and nothing else may work against the store while it runs. A poort is a mountain pass, taken single-file. Unlike a lope it **composes with the designation** rather than foreclosing it: kind and tier are independent axes, so exclusive-and-indult-at-opus is the ordinary shape for schema work. There is no second noun — a pace carrying it is a *poort pace*, and the word is the token. Its whole enforcement is the **operator's word at the mount**: `jjx_orient` prints the confirmation as this kind's protocol line, no lock is taken and no other session is detected, so an unattended session has no path onto one. Full doctrine: JJS0 `jjdpk_poort`.
- **Entrepas**: The third kind — a pace born, worked and closed inside a single dispatch. Its claim is narrow and stated narrowly: **no frontier bridling ruling stood between slate and execution, and no designee warrant governed it** — one authority carried both ends. It never says the work was unreviewed; what it collapses is the ceremony's *ordering*, not its volume. There is no second noun: a pace carrying it is an *entrepas pace*, and the word is the token. Tier is not a parameter of the kind, and the chalk records the tier the session actually ran at. `jjx_apostille` refuses all three forms, `jjx_whorl` refuses the flip in **both** directions, and `jjx_orient` **admits every tier without a warrant** — so the overflow exit is a **new pace, never a mutation**. Its trees stand before you do: the operator opens the **vaquero** door naming a heat and a sire, the stile provisions the whole kraal at dispatch — outspan, work billet, vulgate billet — and lands its charge in the outspan for you to read. What is inverted is the ORDER, never the geometry: the pace those trees are for does not exist until you enroll it, so your first act is that enroll. The trees carry no coronet, none having existed when they were minted; the enroll banks the kraal's serial onto the pace, which is how a remount finds the branch again after this kraal is gone. Full doctrine: JJS0 `jjdpk_entrepas`.
- **Itch**: Future work at any detail level, in jji_itch.md. A **human reminder only** — never load-bearing, never authority. Never cut a pace or implement against itch content as if it were authority; the operator reads it and decides whether it graduates. Load-bearing guidance belongs where agents read it as authority — heat-shape in a paddock, durable facts in a spec.
- **Scar**: Closed work with lessons learned, lives in jjs_scar.md
- **Spook**: Team infrastructure stumble — any workflow failure improvable with deft attention. Capture as a pace when encountered, don't lose the current thread.
- **Cinch**: A decision settled in a paddock or docket and not to be re-litigated — both noun and verb. Distinct from a *lock* in the concurrency sense (JJK's git-ref commit lock), which keeps that word.
- **Phase ladder**: A long arc shaped as a succession of heats, one rung apiece — the ladder recorded in the standing heat's paddock, rungs named but detailed only once their predecessor lands. Three hard laws: a phase boundary is a **slated pace, never paddock prose** (a pace blocks a clean retirement; prose does not); findings travel **verbatim**, proven by byte diff against the source, never re-glossed or condensed; **only live findings travel** — a carry is a liveness filter, and a retired paddock is provenance, never authority. When you groom a heat whose paddock shows a ladder, or cut a heat that will hand work forward, four further rules bind: each rung's done-line is a state predicate to sight in the world, never an adjective and never an ordinal (a rail silently redefines one); the boundary pace nominates the successor heat, carries the live findings into its paddock and expands the next rung into paces; verbatim carriage imports the source's deixis, so the carrying pace frames a carried block — naming its source, the cut, and whose voice its "this heat" speaks in — rather than repairing it, and marks any note it adds as added; and a ladder ends by record, its terminal rung's paddock saying so and slating no boundary pace. Home: JJS0 "The Phase Ladder", cited as depth.
- **Mulling heat**: A stabled heat paired with an aspirant sheaf — aspirancy is declared by the sheaf's `//axvd_sheaf axd_aspirant` voicing, never inferred from prose, and an aspirant may stand *unpaired*, its acronym entry saying which and a heat nominated later when a need calls for one. Design leans bank in the sheaf; work-shape and cinches bank in the paddock — read the paddock before cutting any pace, or the sheaf's settling register where no heat stands. Sheaf-authoring paces may race; implementation paces wait for *settling*, never for infusion. Full doctrine: JJS0 "The Mulling Heat" (Aspirant Nucleations).
**Sincerity over efficiency:** When you notice something — a pattern, a concern, an insight about the work or the collaboration — say it. Discovery through conversation is part of the work, not a detour from it.

**Docket and paddock craft is a pull door.** `Tools/jjk/volde_excursus/volie_slating.md`
holds it — docket posture, the delegate test and a lope's wider done-when,
slate-time versus mount-time, reference discipline, and paddock posture. Read it
before you author a docket or a paddock body. Two dispositions bind earlier than
that read and stay here: a pace whose work needs the operator's word
*mid-execution* is a **lope**, slated `jjx_enroll {kind: "hackamore"}` and never
a rough pace carrying stop-clauses; and a coronet never appears in paddock prose,
silks never in paddock or docket prose.

**The graph question — ask it at every slate, in both directions.** A slate authors edges as well as a docket. Ask first what the born pace must wait on, and name those paces in the birth call's `predecessor_coronets` — plain addressing, since the pace being born is the pace that waits. Then ask the inverse, the direction that gets forgotten: what *standing* pace must now wait on the pace you just slated. Author that edge in the same breath through `jjx_hopple`, whose addressing inverts — its `identity` names the thing waited *on*, its list the dependents that will wait — so one call fans the new work across everything it holds up. Skip the second question and the board lies: a standing terminus whose cinch expects a full leaf set reads *runnable* in the fettle the moment its leaves are slated edge-free among themselves, and stays that way until someone authors the edges by hand. **And the edge now BITES rather than merely showing:** a wait is a condition on starting work and on landing it, never on continuing it, and the rows below carry which door does what. A missing edge therefore costs more than a misleading board — it costs a session dispatched onto work whose inputs have not landed. A wrong edge costs one `jjx_unhopple` on the operator's word, which is the whole of the override; that asymmetry is why an edge is worth authoring on suspicion.

**Stale-by-mount filter.** Each line must still be true at mount-time. Fail (drift): line numbers, byte/match counts, version strings, exhaustive site enumerations. Pass (durable): file paths as entry points, commit hashes, spec/concept names.

**Docket anti-patterns:**
- Restating commits/specs the mount agent already loads. Point, don't paraphrase.
- Pre-baking implementation (function names, signatures, locations for new code). Mount-time decisions.
- Site enumerations. Write a discovery recipe (`grep "pattern" Tools/rbk/`) instead of listing six file:line entries. Where the recipe hunts a name, spell its word boundary with `git grep -w`, never `\b`, which is inert on some stations.
- **Every-X dockets without a census.** A docket that quantifies over a set of code sites ("repoint every mutating command") must carry the set's heterogeneity census or mandate it as the mount's first checkpointed artifact — never open with a binary "confirm whether" first act. The mandate form (discovery recipe + census-first requirement) is the usual fit under the stale-by-mount filter. The census is the plan: a representative-specimen survey excludes by construction the outliers where the risk lives.
- Body over ~15 lines warrants suspicion of overprescription.
- **Plan-step structure stays in the plan.** Docket phase/step labels (A/B/C, "Phase 1", "first/then/finally") must not appear as code comments; line order already conveys execution.
- **Absolute paths to working trees.** Don't pin a docket to a specific repo clone (`/Users/foo/projects/your-project`, `~/proj/`). Mount may legitimately run against a different clone — if work needs to happen in *the* working directory, say so without naming it. Absolute paths *are* acceptable for external data roots (log dirs, `/tmp/...` scratch, machine-pinned data), flagged as operator-mutable rather than identity.

**Semantic linefeeds in artifact prose.** Author paddock and docket prose — the bodies you write into `gazette_in.md` — with *semantic line breaks*: one sentence per line, a long sentence broken at its major internal boundaries (em-dash, semicolon, colon, comma-before-conjunction). Markdown soft-wraps these into one rendered paragraph, so the breaks are invisible in output. Never break at the word level — for word-grain diffs use `git diff --word-diff`, not physically split prose. (Instantiates JJS0's *Diff-Friendly Prose* principle, which governs gazette markdown only — not the `.adoc` specs, whose source answers to MCM line-break discipline.)

**Citation spans in corpus prose.** Corpus prose citing a governed word wears a citation span around it — a backtick literal, an attribute reference in braces, or a cross reference in double angles — since a word standing bare outside one is an ordinary use rather than a citation, however plainly the sentence around it means the name. This binds the specs and guides you edit in the vulgate billet, not the gazette bodies the passage above governs.

**Mid-execution posture.** When a failure or surprise surfaces while a pace is mounted, the default response is mechanism + one specific repair you'd attempt. Do not proliferate options or weigh consequences across alternatives — pace scope and segmentation are operator territory. If the repair is obvious, proceed; else surface the question and stop.

**Identities vs Display Names:**
- **Firemark**: Heat identity, `₣` + two characters from a nonstandard b64 alphabet (e.g., `₣AA`, `₣A-`, `₣A_`, `₣A7`). Used in command params and JSON keys; input may omit the `₣` glyph, output always includes it.
- **Coronet**: Pace identity, `₢` + **five** characters from the same alphabet — one flat global index carrying **no** firemark and encoding **no** heat affiliation (seed-minted coronets begin at `C`, e.g. `₢CAAAG`; the leading characters are never a heat reference, save a grandfathered id whose leading two happen to equal a firemark by frozen coincidence). The **bare five-character body** (`CAAAG`) is the machine identity — command params, JSON keys, git refs. On emission it is qualified by *live* heat affiliation: `₢` + the current heat firemark + the interpunct `·` + the body (`₢B4·CAAAG`, the form seen in prose; a grandfathered pace renders its heat twice, `₢B4·B4AAB`); tables show the bare `₢`+body (`₢CAAAG`). **Any of these forms is accepted as input** — the `₢` glyph and any `·` qualifier are stripped and the bare body is looked up — so you never guess the wire form.
- **Silks**: kebab-case display name. Human-readable only — NOT usable for lookups.

When a command takes a firemark or coronet, provide the identity, not the silks.

**Display discipline (agent text output to user):**
- **Always show the full emitted form; never abbreviate.** Write a coronet
  live-qualified — `₢B4·CAAAG` (`₢` + heat firemark + `·` + body) — and a
  firemark as `₣A-`, never `A-`. A bare body dropped into running prose leaves
  the reader with no heat context. That prefix records *live* affiliation and is
  not part of the immutable identity — a relocate re-qualifies tomorrow's
  rendering — but it is what you display. This holds in prose, tables, bullets,
  headers and casual back-references.
- **Prefer coronets over silks in references.** Coronets are precise and
  immutable; silks change via `jjx_whorl`. Lead with the coronet, silks
  following parenthetically for readability (`₢B4·CAAAG
  (coronet-doc-gestalt-drift)`). The coronet alone is always sufficient; the
  silks alone never are.
- **Say *lope* for the pace, `hackamore` for the token.** In prose to the
  operator a pace carrying that kind is a lope — never "a hackamore pace". Use
  the token only where it is literally spelled: a param value (`{kind:
  "hackamore"}`), a word the equerry printed, a JSON value. Use both words in one
  breath the first time they meet in a session, so a reader told they hold a
  lope can still reach a value spelled `hackamore`.
- **Poort has no second noun, so say *poort pace*.** *Lope* and `hackamore` are
  two words for one thing; poort is one word for one thing, and inventing a noun
  for it would be minting in prose. It is the pace's kind, never its state — so
  never write it where a state word belongs.

**MCP Tool Usage:**

All JJK commands are accessed via the single `mcp__vvx__jjx` MCP tool with three parameters — the whole per-call surface, with nothing else accepted:
- `command`: string selecting the operation — always the canonical `jjx_*` name (e.g., `"jjx_show"`, `"jjx_record"`)
- `params`: JSON object with command-specific fields (see reference below)
- `model`: agent's verbatim model ID string from its system prompt (e.g., `"claude-opus-4-8"`). Required on ALL commands.

**There is no session-identity parameter, and no verb that mints one.** The door seated this server in the kraal it serves, so its own working directory already tells it whose tenure this is. Pass `command`, `params` and `model`; nothing more is read. Why the parameter is declined rather than merely unused: JJS0 "Session Exchange".

**`params` must be a JSON object, never a string.** If params is accidentally stringified (e.g., `"{\"key\": \"val\"}"` instead of `{"key": "val"}`), deserialization will fail. The server has a defensive fallback for this, but always pass a native object.

**Interdictum — the gating refusal.** A jjx result whose first word is `INTERDICTUM` bars the act; it is not a *vitium* — the correctable verdict, which you fix and retry. The genre and its severity rule are spec-homed at JJS0 `jjdz_interdictum` — the three verdicts (monitum, interdictum, vitium), the differentia that sorts them (a vitium is correctable by fixing the call; an interdictum's condition lies outside it), and the emission's own obligations. A vitium carries no wire token of its own and none is coming, so the recognition rule stays exactly one: the leading `INTERDICTUM`, or its absence. Cited, not summarized: what binds *you* is the three bullets below, and an agent never needs the spec to obey a token. It is named because the author of the next refusal does need it.

- **Stop the act and its objective by any route** — no altered-params retry, no other command to the same end, and no gathering of context to hand the operator a recommendation the interdictum did not invite. What is barred is not only the literal act but everything downstream that served it, a substitute recommendation included; a *read-only* read pursued to that end is the workaround, not an exempt route. The rationalization "reads aren't to the same end" is exactly the loophole this shuts. Unrelated work continues.
- **Report it verbatim, and stop there.** The message is self-sufficient: what refused, why, the remedies. The verbatim report is your whole response — do nothing further unless the operator directs it, and pursue a named remedy only on their word — save the refit a refused wrap demands, which Commit Discipline's staleness law has you take unasked.
- **Do not model the guard.** This context says nothing about what generates an interdictum — carried mechanism becomes a self-check, and second implementations drift. Attempt the act; obey the token.

**What each door DOES is a pull door.** The table above is the param contract
and stops there; `Tools/jjk/volde_excursus/volie_portolan.md` carries every
command's behavior. Read it before you drive a verb for the first time this
session, and whenever one of these plain questions comes up:

- *what does this print, and what rides the gazette* — which half of a result is
  inline, what a count counts, what a bounded index clipped;
- *what does this param do, and what if I leave it out* — whether a list is a
  filter or an election, what absent means beside `[]`, what a field replaces;
- *what does this notice need* — which slug, whether it takes a lede, what the
  body must carry;
- *what does this refuse, and why did it just refuse me* — the refusal roster
  per door, which the signature line does not carry;
- *what does this void, announce, or leave alone* — designations, waits,
  deferrals, criteria already driven.

A refusal you have already met names its own remedy. The portolan is for the
call you have not made yet.

The six remote commands (`jjx_bind`, `jjx_send`, `jjx_plant`, `jjx_relay`, `jjx_check`, `jjx_fetch`) are deliberately absent above — they are catalogued in `Tools/jjk/volde_excursus/volie_foray.md`, which the Foray Protocol routes you to before the first call.

### Session Protocol

**There is no opening ceremony — begin working.** A session performs no startup act, mints nothing, and holds no session token: your first jjx call is whatever the work needs, usually the `jjx_orient` that mounts your pace. Nothing has to be threaded through later calls.

A chat the door did not dispatch holds no jjx tools at all, the MCP config being written per-dispatch, and is refused outright with an INTERDICTUM naming both doors if one is reached anyway. The law and its ground: JJS0 "Session Exchange".

**The exchange is the kraal's seat: `gazette_in.md` and `gazette_out.md` stand in this session's own working directory.** They are not in a per-session directory somewhere else, and there is nothing to derive from an id.

**Take the paths from the equerry, never from memory.** `jjx_orient` prints the absolute pair beneath the mount; a setter that finds nothing staged names the exact path it checked. Use what you are given verbatim. Do not rebuild a path from a remembered layout — a notice written to the wrong directory does not fail as a wrong gazette, it fails as an absent one, and the refusal will name a path you did not write to.

Every jjx MCP call the dispatcher admits deletes both gazette files on entry (read+delete `gazette_in.md`, delete `gazette_out.md`) — gazette content has single-MCP-call lifetime, a parameter or return value, not persistent state. A handful of refusals stand *ahead* of that entry read and leave a staged notice untouched; see **On failure** below.

**⚠️ `gazette_in.md` is the argument to the *next* jjx call — whichever fires next consumes it on entry, not a particular command.** So write the gazette as the immediate last step before *that* call; if you reconsider which command to run, rewrite or clear it first. **Deleted-on-entry means read-on-entry** — a stale notice is consumed as this call's input *before* it is deleted, and a gazette *setter* writes and **auto-commits with no confirm gate**. Keep every gazette write welded to the single call it was written for.

- **`gazette_in.md`** (agent → server): write before a setter (`jjx_enroll`, `jjx_redocket`, `jjx_curry`, `jjx_rodear`), before `jjx_apostille` (its **required** `jjezs_warrant` on the tier form, `jjezs_abeyance` on the deferral form), before `jjx_archive` (its **required** `jjezs_quittance`), or before `jjx_orient` / `jjx_show` (their `jjezs_halter` target selection). For every one of them the gazette is the sole input path — there is no JSON param fallback.
- **`gazette_out.md`** (server → agent): written by getter commands, read after they return. Notices are `# jjezs_paddock <firemark>` / `# jjezs_pace <coronet>` / `# jjezs_steeplechase <firemark>`, content beneath each: `jjx_orient` → paddock, the mounted pace whole (original-intent, then docket, then pace digest), and the recent-work entries; `jjx_show` → paddock(s) + a docket per resolved pace, each carrying its warrant beneath where the pace has one; `jjx_paddock` → paddock only; `jjx_cavvy` → the registry's canonical body under a LEDE-LESS `# jjezs_cavvy` header (the cavvy is a single store-level document with no identity to name); `jjx_log` → every steeplechase entry with its subject untruncated (the inline table clips subjects, so this is the only full reading); `jjx_gesta` → every harvested wrap under a LEDE-LESS `# jjezs_gesta` header (a harvest spans every heat and names no identity), each carrying its summary whole and its W chalk's trailers verbatim — the only channel the trailers have at all; `jjx_search` → the whole result document under a LEDE-LESS `# jjezs_harbourage` header (a search spans every heat and names no identity), the inline result being a bounded index into it.

**Never reach past the JJK interface to raw storage — NO exceptions.** Do not parse the harness's persisted tool-result files or the studbook-journaled gallops JSON directly. `gazette_out.md` is what a getter wrote for you: read it. To read one pace's docket, call `jjx_brief {coronet}` (returns inline), and loop it per pace if a large `jjx_show` overflows.

**Gazette wire format (setter commands):**
Each notice is a `#`-header line with slug and lede, followed by a content body. The lede is **exactly one whitespace-free token** (silks / coronet / firemark, per the table below) — nothing follows it on the `#` line; the body goes beneath, and appending extra text to the lede folds it into the identity and fails validation. `jjezs_halter` is body-less (the lede is the whole notice); the content-bearing slugs (`jjezs_slate`, `jjezs_reslate`, `jjezs_paddock`, `jjezs_dictation`, `jjezs_precis`, and the three lede-less ones, `jjezs_warrant`, `jjezs_cavvy` and `jjezs_quittance`) require a **non-empty** body — an empty or whitespace-only body rejects loud, a setter never executes a clear.

**Critical: `#` (H1) in gazette_in.md is a wire format delimiter, NOT a markdown heading.** For single-notice commands (enroll, curry, orient), use exactly ONE `#` line. For mass reslate and multi-target show, each `# jjezs_reslate` / `# jjezs_halter` line starts a new notice. All markdown headings within body content must use `##` or deeper — a bare `#` line inside content is parsed as a notice boundary, and rejects naming the offending line and slug (`Line 5: unknown slug 'Paddock:'`).

**On failure: re-write `gazette_in.md` from scratch before retrying, whatever refused you.** Most refusals reach you from *past* the entry read — every parse, param and validation refusal among them — so the notice is already consumed and a blind retry sends an empty gazette. A few stand *ahead* of it and leave the notice standing untouched: a verb this equerry does not carry, a model-gate refusal, the doorless bar, and a malformed call the transport turns away before the dispatcher runs at all. That distinction is the equerry's and not yours to act on: nothing in a refusal tells you which side of the read it fell on, so the re-write law is unconditional and errs safe under either. Never retry on the strength of a notice you believe survived.

**A call that dies with no verdict at all is a different thing from a refusal, and for `jjx_refit` and `jjx_testimonium` the remedy is simply to re-run it.**
A dropped transport — `Connection closed` or any dead call returning neither success nor refusal — leaves you unable to tell from the call whether the act landed, and neither door asks you to find out: a refit whose axis already advanced reports that axis already current, and a wrap whose converge landed before its journal was refused says so and resumes on the re-run, converging nothing a second time.
Re-run the door and read what it reports; do not go reading the trees by hand to reconstruct what happened.

**Original intent at slate.** Every slate stages the dictation and precis
companions beside its slate notice — wire form in the table above. Both freeze
at slate and no verb rewrites them, so an omission can never be repaired.
Optional at the tool, required at every slate. There is no exception: every
pace is born at `jjx_enroll`, so an intent-less pace is one whose slater omitted
the capture, never one whose door could not hold it.

**Gazette paths are emitted by the server** — `jjx_orient` returns the absolute `gazette_in`/`gazette_out` pair; use them verbatim, never rebuilt and never searched for (single-MCP-call lifetime means a search finds nothing or something stale). A setter that finds nothing staged names the exact path it checked, so the paths are recoverable even in a session that never mounts.

**Read-modify-write workflow** (paddock editing):
1. Call `jjx_paddock {firemark}` → writes `gazette_out.md`, carrying the paddock notice and a `jjezs_cocket` notice beside it
2. Rename `gazette_out.md` → `gazette_in.md`, edit the paddock body — **leave the cocket notice alone and in place**
3. Call `jjx_curry`

**Compose the body in scratch before you stage it.** Any refusal consumes the staged notice, so a revision authored straight into `gazette_in.md` is gone the moment the write is turned away — and the retry is a re-authoring from memory rather than a re-read and a re-insert from a file you still hold.

The registry takes the same shape — `jjx_cavvy` → edit → `jjx_rodear` — the emitted notice being byte-identical to the one the writer expects. **But never begin it unbidden: see Rodear Protocol below.**

**Mass reslate is a pull door.** When one mechanical edit has to reach many dockets at once, read `Tools/jjk/volde_excursus/volie_reslate.md` for the show → reslate bridge before improvising a run.

### Parallel Tool Batch Discipline

When tool calls share one parallel block and one exits non-zero, the harness **cancels the still-queued siblings** ("Cancelled: parallel tool call … errored"), so a screenful of cancellations usually traces to *one* real error upstream — diagnose that, don't react to the cascade. Two rules:

- **Never co-batch a producer with its consumer.** A command and another that needs its output, or a path built from it, must be sequenced across tool blocks. `jjx_record` alongside the edits/checks that must *precede* it is the standing case: verify the tree first, commit second. A gazette write and the single call that consumes it are the same shape — write, then call, never both in one block.
- **Keep fragile or dependent commands out of wide read-only batches** — an empty/failed sibling can silently cancel unrelated reads.

### Mount Protocol

When user says "mount" or you need to engage the next pace:

1. Resolve the target: if the user says "mount" without specifying a heat and you have no prior heat context this session, ask which heat rather than guessing; if you have prior context (previously mounted/groomed heat), use that firemark. Write exactly one `# jjezs_halter <firemark|coronet>` notice to `gazette_in.md`, then run `jjx_orient {}`.
2. Parse output: the `Standing:`/`Protocol:` verdict lines lead it, then Heat/Paddock/Next, then the warrant if the pace carries one. Everything else — paddock, original-intent, docket, pace digest, recent work — is in `gazette_out.md`; read it. A staleness warning naming `jjx_refit` may stand ahead of `Heat:`. **Read `gazette_out.md` before you refit** — the refit clears the gazette on entry as every jjx call does (JJS0 "Session Exchange"), so a refit taken first destroys the payload this mount just wrote. Then remedy it yourself before you propose an approach, exactly as Commit Discipline below directs for a staleness line. After a refit that landed, re-stage the halter and orient again: what you read described the pre-merge tree, and a twice-mounted pace is already lawful.
3. **Read the paddock before the docket.** The paddock tells you the shape of the work; the docket tells you what to do next. An `Original-intent` block above the docket is the slate-frozen understanding; the docket below is the living authority.
4. **Lead with the pace goal in one sentence**, distilled from the docket — what this pace is trying to accomplish, stated precisely (paces can sit weeks between slating and mounting; goal recall comes first). Then display brief heat context (silks + paddock one-liner + recent work) and finally the full docket.
5. **Run the readiness half of the bridling procedure**, which a rough pace
   mounted at full ceremony has never passed:
   `Tools/jjk/volde_excursus/volie_bridling.md` — the trees, the three gap
   classes, the lope test, and the done-proof litmus — skipping tier, effort
   and warrant and recording nothing. A gap is a reslate need: surface it and
   stop rather than improvising past it.
6. **Name assessment**: If pace silks doesn't fit docket, offer a dight via `jjx_whorl {coronet, silks}`
7. Propose the approach compactly — a one-line recommended execution strategy (model tier + sequential/parallel). Offer the reasoning (tier rationale, parallelization analysis) on request, not unprompted. Tiers: haiku (mechanical), sonnet (standard dev), opus (architectural).
8. Ask to proceed, then begin work — a designee skips the ask, the launch that seated it being the proceed

### Standing and Protocols

**Your standing on a mount is decided by the server, never by you.** `jjx_orient`
reads the `model` param you pass and prints two lines — `Standing:` and
`Protocol:` — at the very *head* of its result, ahead of everything else it
emits, so they cannot be scrolled past. Obey them. Do not weigh your own model
against the pace's tier, and do not reason about which protocol you fall under:
a mount that reaches you has already been judged, and a mount you may not make
is refused outright rather than returned for you to interpret. Who closes is the
launch's attendance, never your tier: attended, the operator wraps at every tier;
only an unattended launch licenses a warranted session to wrap itself on green.

This law binds from mount through close, in every working session. It is not
bridling craft and is never deferred: the sessions it governs most completely
are the ones that exercise no judgment about their own constraints. The two
protocols orient names:

- **Full ceremony** — mount, work, and wrap the pace yourself (the Wrap
  Discipline below still governs: ask the operator before wrapping).
- **Lope** — a pace carrying the hackamore kind (JJS0 `jjdpk_hackamore`): it
  completes in conversation with the operator rather than by execution. `jjx_orient`
  prints the protocol whole when you mount one; obey what it printed. Two things it
  does not carry: the Wrap Discipline below still governs the exit that finishes
  cheap and wraps, and a premise that breaks is a lope **completing**, not failing.

**The entrepas kind displaces those two, as a lope does, and for the mirror
reason.** A lope's verdict describes how a session OPENS, the pace never being
executed; an entrepas verdict describes how a session CLOSES, every other
protocol assuming the pace outlives the session reading it where here it does
not. You hold full ceremony at whatever tier you are — no
warrant governs the pace and none can, so the tier moves your Standing line and
nothing else. `jjx_orient` prints the rest, the obligation to resolve the pace
before this chat ends among it.

**The poort kind is a gate over those two, never a third.** Where a lope
*replaces* your protocol, a poort pace *gates* it: the verdict names the kind and
then hands you whichever of the two your designation earns, and `jjx_orient`
prints the confirmation the gate requires. Once the operator confirms, proceed
under the protocol the verdict gave you, unchanged.

### Bridle Protocol

**Excursus — read `Tools/jjk/volde_excursus/volie_bridling.md` before you bridle, whenever
bridling or warrants are under discussion.** It carries the designation
procedure whole: the readiness judgment, how to locate the tier, the
mechanization weighing, the warrant's authoring discipline and wire format,
and a worked example. Never bridle from memory of it — the tier you rule and
the criteria you write are both licences you are granting.

Bridling designates a pace for execution at a specific model tier. A frontier
agent — always the calling agent, never the server — makes **three distinct
judgments** and records them together: that the docket is **ready to execute**,
**which tier** that execution needs, and **what would prove the work done** —
the warrant's criteria. Readiness is the gate; the tier is the ruling; the
warrant is the licence. A `rough` pace is one no frontier agent has ruled on
yet; a `indult` pace carries its tier (and optionally an effort word) until it
is released, reverted, or closed.

The dispositions below stay resident here, because each binds before or apart
from the moment the excursus is read:

- **Rough is the absence of a ruling, never a signal.** A pace whose execution
  needs opus judgment is bridled *at opus* — never left rough to say so, since
  rough reads identically to a pace nobody has examined. Bridling at a frontier
  tier is a first-class act: the only durable record that a frontier agent read
  this docket and ruled it frontier-grade, the only thing that surfaces the tier
  in `jjx_coronets` (`[indult opus]`), and the only way to record an effort
  word. Its mirror binds equally: "this pace needs judgment to execute" is not a
  gap and is never a reason to withhold designation. Both temptations are toward
  *not* designating — an inaction that announces no occasion, so no trigger
  fires and neither can wait behind the door.
- **Prove the docket's work against the trees a dispatch would stand up, before
  you rule the tier.** Name the trees the work reads and writes, and answer each
  against the sire block a `jjx_show` at the pace's coronet renders: the write
  sire, the declared read list, and what registration adds. A tree the work
  needs and none of the three provides is a refusal to designate, and the remedy
  is the read-list edit (`jjx_whorl {coronet, readable_sires}`) — which must
  land BEFORE the bridle, because that field voids a designation. This binds
  ahead of the excursus read because of how the failure it catches presents: a
  designee handed a docket that reads a tree its kraal was never given meets no
  refusal, it meets an ABSENCE, and an absence is what an agent improvises
  around.
- **A designation is void when its judgment inputs change, and the voiding
  table is the whole rule.** Four authored fields void: the **docket** (the
  text the judgment read), the **sire** (which tree a saddle provisions), the
  **read list** (which further trees it provisions), and the **kind** (what the
  work IS). Two never do: **silks** and **the waits** — so `jjx_hopple` and
  `jjx_unhopple`, the only doors that write a wait after birth at either grain, cost a
  designation nothing and need no weighing before you reach for them. Transfer and relocate void as well;
  reorder does not. A void reverts the pace to rough and wipes the tier,
  effort and warrant together.
  Where the void is announced differs by door, and the difference is a live
  hazard rather than a nicety: `jjx_whorl` names the tier it discarded, so a
  sire, read-list or kind edit says what it cost — but a **redocket announces
  nothing**, so refining a docket still discards its designation silently. That
  binds at *reslate*, not at bridling.
- **A lope is not a bridle candidate, and its docket's open question is not a
  gap.** `jjx_apostille` refuses a pace carrying the hackamore kind outright,
  under its own sentence naming the kind and the `jjx_whorl` flip back — so
  the refusal reaches you whether or not you read the excursus. What the
  excursus adds, and what binds *before* you designate, is the other
  direction: a pace standing rough whose real work is the operator
  conversation should be reported as a lope rather than refused as unready.
  Unlike the two above, the kind survives a redocket: it rides its own field,
  which the reslate write cannot reach.
- **An entrepas is not a bridle candidate either, and its refusal names no
  remedy on that pace.** `jjx_apostille` refuses all three forms outright, so the
  refusal reaches you whether or not you read the excursus. What parts it from
  the lope is what to do next: a lope's bar is liftable, the flip being a
  legitimate graduation, and this one is not — the kind refuses the flip in both
  directions. So never report an entrepas as a pace wanting a designation. Work
  that outgrew one closes as what it is, and the real work is slated as a **new
  pace**, which is then an ordinary bridle candidate.
- **A poort pace IS a bridle candidate, and the two axes compose.** Do not read
  the bullet above as a rule about the kind axis: it is a rule about one value.
  `jjx_apostille` refuses a poort pace nothing, the rough filter keeps it, and
  exclusive-and-indult-at-opus is the ordinary shape for schema work. Bridle it
  on the docket's merits and let the kind alone; the kind gates the mount, which
  is a different question from what tier the execution needs.

### Rodear Protocol

**Registry edits are the operator's. Never call `jjx_rodear` on your own
initiative** — recommend, and stop.

The fence stands on **initiative, not on the verb**, exactly as heat birth and
death do. Propose freely: a sweep that finds an unregistered BUK-like tree and
recommends registering it is valuable and costs nothing. Never execute unbidden.
The rationale is that a registration error is a durably misfiled record that
surfaces months later, and one saved turn is a bad trade against that.

registry's shape, the grade and transport rosters, the verdict words, and the
propose-then-stop workflow. Read it before recommending or performing a
registry edit; it is a pull door, outside the standing include tree, because
sessions that need this vocabulary are rare.

### Commit Discipline

When working on a heat, use `jjx_record` for commits with heat/pace affiliation.

`files` has three readings, and **omitted is not the same as `[]`**:

**Whole dirty tree** (the default — omit `files`):
Use `jjx_record` with `{identity: "CORONET", intent: "description"}`. Everything dirty in the billet rides, as at wrap. Prefer this: work left out of a list stays dirty and later rides the wrap under a machine-written message instead of your intent.

**Exactly these paths** (pass a list):
Use `jjx_record` with `{identity: "CORONET", files: ["file1", "file2"], intent: "description"}`. Use it to bank a coherent slice deliberately — a mechanical sweep apart from a design change. Anything dirty and unlisted is named back to you in a warning.

**Heat-affiliated commit** (no active pace, but part of heat work):
Use `jjx_record` with `{identity: "FIREMARK", ...}` — the same three readings apply.

**Empty commit** (work that changed nothing on disk — a verification run, a platform proof):
Use `jjx_record` with `files: []` explicitly. The commit lands, affiliated, carrying `intent` as its whole content; a warning names it afterward. `intent` is required here — there is no diff to describe.

Synthesize intent from the conversation — describe *what* was accomplished, not *how*.

**A notch after this kraal's own wrap is demoted, not refused.** A settled pace owns no further work, so naming its coronet lands the commit affiliated to its **heat** instead, and says so. Nothing is lost: the commit stands on the billet branch and reaches remote custody with it. What it has is no pace to carry it — so **slate one and name that commit in its docket**. The monitum you read in the moment is the only place the debt is recorded; nothing greps for it later.

**Two staleness lines, and both name `jjx_refit`.** The mount reports a billet behind trunk's remote counterpart, and `jjx_record` reports it again on every commit — the mount is one reading at one instant, and the notch is the backstop for trunk moving while you work. Both are monita: they report, you remedy. Act on the first one you see: a refit is cheapest before your work piles onto a stale base, and a twice-mounted pace inherits its first mount's fork point, so a mount is not a first sighting. Where the line you saw was the mount's, read the mount payload first — `gazette_out.md` is cleared on the refit's entry like any other call's — then orient again once the merge has landed, for a payload describing the tree you now hold.

**What you owe either line is the same at every tier: attempt the refit, and obey the verdict.** The verb is open to you whatever your standing. Don't weigh your own tier before calling it, and don't retry or route around a refusal. **A refit voids criteria you have already driven**, so re-drive them on the merged tree before you close.

**A second staleness line in the same pace surfaces to the operator before you refit, naming which criteria you have already driven** — the cost the operator is then authorizing, not only the act itself. **The third line and every later one surface the same way**, each naming the criteria standing driven at that moment: the surfacing is per line rather than once per pace, since a window now holds four refits and the cost the operator is authorizing grows with every one. Two exceptions carry no threshold and refit unasked regardless: a pace the staleness itself blocks outright, and a refit a refused wrap demands.

When user says "notch", determine context (pace or heat affiliated) and invoke `jjx_record` with the appropriate identity.

**Where a spec edit goes.** Specs and guides are edited in the kraal's **vulgate billet** — the corpus work tree standing beside your work billet, on this pace's own branch — and recorded with `jjx_record {vulgate: true}`, which commits and consigns to remote custody in one act, so each notch is custody at once; the wrap converges what is committed onto the corpus trunk, and a corpus-billet-behind line on a mount or a notch means run `jjx_refit` now, while your pace context is live. Full law: JJSVS-studbook.adoc "Knowledge homing"; the converge, the landing gate and the `Vulgate:` trailer are JJSRWP-wrap.adoc; the billet choreography, refit's corpus axis and the stile's clearing are JJSVD-dispatch.adoc; the design home is JJSAE-embillet.adoc.

**Where your estate keeps a name census, its customs are a pull door.** Where the outspan you stand in carries `volie_custumal.md`, this estate runs a census tool beside Job Jockey, and that door says when a docket's recipe, a warrant's criterion or a wrap's evidence reads the census instead of a search, and how each reading is taken — read it the first time any of them comes due.
